# Experiment E1 on the pilot data (evaluate_static.py), run on 2026-11-22

PROVISIONAL and NOT a result for the report: training user u99 (one person, near, plain background, bright
light; fist, open palm, thumb up, thumb down), plus the open-palm clips of u01 (the SAME person's hand,
another session). No point and no no_gesture recordings exist yet. With one person and four gestures far
apart, test 1 is at the ceiling for almost every method; the useful parts are tests 2 and 3.

UPDATE 2026-11-23: the person who recorded them confirmed that u01 open_palm clips 2, 3 and 5 were thumb-up
poses. They were removed from data/landmarks/u01/open_palm.json and the remaining clips renumbered so the
validator accepts the file: old 1 -> 1, old 4 -> 2, old 6 -> 3 (the other clips are unchanged byte for byte).
The output at the end of this file is from BEFORE the removal and uses the old numbers. After the removal:
train_static.py labels all 59 u01 frames open_palm (100 %); in test 3 of evaluate_static.py raw63 (and +rot)
reach 100 % and the rules and all rotation-independent SVMs 95 %. The difference is 3 frames (8 to 10) at the
start of clip 1: all four fingers are bent there (sum of joint bends about 2.8 rad), so the hand was closed
for a moment before it opened, and the loader kept the frames because the hand was still. The rules call them
fist, raw63 open_palm (matches the label, not the shape) and inv+orient +rot thumb_down with confidence 0.94,
i.e. the learned model is confidently wrong on a fist that looks unlike the training fists. The loader's
stable-frame selection checks stillness only, not that the pose is the labelled one.

Reproduce with:  .venv\Scripts\python.exe evaluate_static.py
(Python 3.13.11, numpy 2.5.5, scikit-learn 1.9.1 on this machine; the pilot of 2026-11-21 used Python 3.10 and
scikit-learn 1.7.2. train_static.py gives the same numbers as on 2026-11-21.)

Methods: rules = rule_classifier.py (finger extended/bent from joint bend angles, thumb from tip-to-index-base
distance, thumb direction for up/down; thresholds fitted on the training frames). SVM = train_static.LandmarkSVM
(RBF, standardised inputs, sigmoid-calibrated probabilities with clip-wise held-out parts). Feature sets from
hand_features.py: raw63 (normalised coordinates), invariant (15 joint bends + 13 distances, unchanged by
turning the hand in the picture), inv+orient (invariant + vertical thumb direction). "+rot" = training frames
of fist / open palm (/ point / no gesture) added 3 times, turned by a random angle within +-90 degrees.

Findings
1. CORRECTION to static_classifier_pilot_2026-11-21.txt. u01 open_palm clips 2, 3 and 5 are not open palms:
   in all their frames the four fingers are bent as in a fist (sum of joint bends about 3.8 rad, open palm
   about 0.3) and the thumb is extended upwards; a drawing of the landmarks looks like u99's thumb_up clips.
   They are almost certainly thumb-up poses recorded under the label open_palm (to be confirmed by replaying
   the clips). The "wrong" thumb_up answers of the first classifier on these clips were therefore mostly
   RIGHT. Every method with rotation-independent features, and the rules, call all three clips thumb_up and
   the three real open palms open_palm (except 3 transition frames of clip 1 with bent fingers). The raw63
   SVM scores highest against the recorded labels (78 %) only because on clip 2 (tilt 39 deg) it makes the
   same mistake as the label. Accuracy against these labels must not be quoted.
2. The orientation problem is nevertheless real (test 2): turning fist and open-palm test frames makes the
   raw63 SVM collapse (93 % at +30 deg, 16 % at +60 deg, 0 % at +90 deg; the wrong answers are thumb_down and
   open_palm), and its wrong answers come with high confidence (at threshold 0.9 only 22 % of them are held
   back). The rules and every SVM on rotation-independent features stay at 100 % at all angles.
3. Rotation augmentation alone does not rescue raw63: it fixes the turned frames but in test 1 it now calls
   one thumb_down clip a fist (41 frames), because a fist turned by about 90 degrees has the coordinates of
   a thumb down. With inv+orient the thumb distance keeps the two apart.
4. Recommendation for 4.6, to be confirmed with more users: SVM on inv+orient with rotation augmentation as
   the learned model, the rules as the E1 baseline. The pilot cannot separate rules from SVM (both 100 %);
   that needs several users, point and no_gesture data, and hand orientations that vary.
Limits: one hand; only rotation in the picture plane was simulated (not turning towards or away from the
camera); test 2 turns recorded frames artificially, which is not the same as recording tilted hands.
